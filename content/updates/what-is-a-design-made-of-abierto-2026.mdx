*Mexico City, Oct 1, 2026* — The Sustainable Design Standard is on display at Centro Nacional de las Artes until October 4th, as part of the 10th Abierto de Diseño CDMX, themed “¿De qué está hecho un diseño? / What Is a Design Made Of?” The interactive installation explores the environmental, social, cultural, and financial impacts created by design decisions. It uses its exhibition project as a real-world test of its framework — a system developed over more than a decade by designers working across Mexico, Canada, and Germany.

Decisions involved in producing the exhibition, from material quantities and printing to fabrication, energy, labour, accessibility, transportation, cost, reuse, and end-of-life planning, are considered through the same criteria that the SD Standard asks other designers to apply.

> “Every design, whether a brochure, a website, or a designed product, is part of a much larger system,” says Alex Quinto, co-author of the Sustainable Design Standard. “We want designers to consider not only what something is made of, but who makes it, who can use it, what resources it depends on, what impact it has, what value it creates, and what happens when it is no longer needed.”

A central part of the installation was developed in collaboration with Mexico City design studio Arudeko, bringing material experimentation, textile production, artisan knowledge, and local making directly into the design process. Arudeko was selected to co-produce part of the installation because they bridge traditional handmade craftsmanship with contemporary design. For the SD Standard and Arudeko, the installation makes design decisions, evidence, uncertainties, and compromises more visible.

The collaboration allowed the project to examine key attributes of sustainable design practice.

> “We see design and production as more than materials. They are about relationships, skills, knowledge, traditions, and creating value within a community. This project gave us a chance to bring those ideas into a broader conversation about sustainable design,” says Denisse Arnaiz, Co-founder of Arudeko design studio.

Visitors can encounter the Standard’s four pillars, explore individual criteria, and use digital tools that extend the experience beyond the exhibition until October 4th. The Sustainable Design Standard is a continually evolving framework inviting designers to ask more questions and find more solutions to today’s critical issues.

**About the Sustainable Design Standard**

The Sustainable Design Standard (SD Standard) is an open-source framework that enables designers to identify, assess, and reduce the environmental, social, cultural, and financial impacts of their work. Created over 15 years with input from designers across the globe, SDS provides practical criteria to guide decisions, explore alternatives, and measure progress. Its long-term goal is to make sustainability assessment a routine part of professional design practice—from the brief and design process through production, use, and end of life.
