The [Sustainable Design Standard](/about) was selected to participate in the 10th [Bienal Iberoamericana de Diseño (BID26)](https://bid-dimad.com/), held November 2–7, 2026, at Central de Diseño in Matadero Madrid. The selection followed the SD Standard’s [installation at Abierto de Diseño CDMX](/projects/abierto) and marked another opportunity to present the project as an open framework for understanding and improving design’s impacts.

Under the theme *Prácticas en transformación*, BID26 approached design through the processes, systems, and relationships that shape social, cultural, environmental, and technological contexts. In addition to being included in the Bienal, the SD Standard took part in two activities that asked how designers can better understand and evaluate the consequences of their work.

## Looking beyond individual design projects

On November 3, the *Diálogo de saberes* [“¿Qué estamos diseñando realmente?”](https://bid-dimad.com/evento/que-estamos-disenando-realmente/) brought together Sebastián Gagin of Studio Gagin, Argentina; circular-economy specialist Raquel Ariza, director of ECOINNOVA, Argentina; Felipe Borbón of Fundación Apapacho, Colombia; and Alejandro Quinto of the Sustainable Design Standard, Mexico, as interlocutor. The conversation took place in the Sala de Columnas at Central de Diseño.

Drawing on perspectives from urban information systems, circular economy, service design, and sustainable communication design, the discussion looked beyond individual objects or communication pieces. It considered what designers are responsible for when their work influences public infrastructure, institutions, social relationships, and everyday experiences.

The SD Standard contributed a framework for examining the [environmental, social, cultural, and financial consequences of visual communication projects](/explore/pillars). At the centre of the discussion was a practical question: **How can designers understand whether the systems they help create produce positive outcomes for people and the planet?**

## Putting the Standard to the test

On November 4, the public clinic [“Pon tu proyecto a prueba”](https://bid-dimad.com/evento/pon-tu-proyecto-a-prueba/) demonstrated how the Standard can be used with real projects. In the Galería at Central de Diseño, Alejandro Quinto applied its environmental, social, cultural, and financial criteria in conversation with the participating designers.

The session examined two distinct projects:

- **YKUA — Laura Giucich, Paraguay.** A series of illuminated objects inspired by Paraguay’s natural water sources, combining wheel-thrown ceramic bases made with kaolin and clay associated with Areguá, glass associated with Itauguá, and integrated lighting.
- **Árboles para la naturaleza — TEMBOL, Puerto Rico.** A digital resource developed for Para la Naturaleza that brings together photographs, illustrations, audio, and species profiles to help people identify native trees and shrubs and make informed reforestation decisions.

The exercise was not a certification, formal approval, or simple assignment of sustainability scores. It helped identify design decisions that may contribute to positive outcomes, recognise trade-offs between sustainability considerations, and compare the questions raised by a physical product with those raised by a digital communication tool. It also exposed gaps and limitations in the Standard’s current criteria. In this way, assessment became part of an iterative design process: the projects tested the framework while the framework helped participants examine the projects.

## What comes next

Together, the two events moved from principles to practice. The first encouraged a systemic view of design’s influence; the second showed how the Standard can structure a collaborative examination of real work without making unverified claims about its outcomes.

These experiences reinforce the project’s ambition to make sustainability assessment more accessible, actionable, and relevant to everyday design decisions. The SD Standard continues to develop its criteria, Knowledge Base, and digital tools. Designers, studios, educators, and organisations are invited to [explore the SD Standard Workspace](/workspace) and consider participating in future pilot activities.
