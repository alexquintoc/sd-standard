Planetary Communications is a communication framework and handbook developed by the Stockholm Resilience Centre to help researchers, communicators, designers, and organizations communicate sustainability science more effectively in an increasingly complex and polarized information environment. Drawing inspiration from the Planetary Boundaries framework and the Centre’s experience communicating interdisciplinary research, the project was developed with contributions from more than one hundred researchers, communicators, and creatives.

The framework identifies nine ingredients for effective sustainability communication: be factful, simplify, time it wisely, link environmental issues to other societal concerns, make communication actionable, highlight solutions, navigate emotions, make it personal, and visualize it. Rather than prescribing a single formula, the framework treats these ingredients like an equalizer: every communication effort should consider all nine, but their relative emphasis should change according to the audience, message, medium, and context.

For communication designers, the framework is particularly useful because it connects scientific accuracy with questions of accessibility, storytelling, visualization, emotion, cultural relevance, and action. It encourages designers to think beyond simply presenting information and instead consider how communication can help audiences understand complex systems, see their relationship to environmental challenges, imagine alternatives, and identify meaningful ways to act.

Planetary Communications therefore complements the SD Standard’s broader approach to sustainable visual communication. While the SD Standard considers the environmental, social, cultural, and financial impacts of design itself, Planetary Communications offers a useful framework for thinking about the effectiveness and responsibility of sustainability messages: not only what we communicate, but how communication can contribute to understanding and constructive action.
