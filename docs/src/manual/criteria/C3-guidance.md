# C3: INDIGENOUS CULTURE — Extended Guidance

Related criterion: [C3: INDIGENOUS CULTURE](../../generated/criteria/CCP2.md)

## What cultural projects other than language can be used?

Culture can show itself in many forms include traditional dress, craftwork, music, dance or any other cultural activity recognized as indigenous culture, whether traditional or modern-day.

## How is aboriginal or indigenous defined?

The Working Definition of Indigenous People Proposed by the UN Working Group on Indigenous Populations is: “…those which, having a historical continuity with pre-invasion and precolonial societies that developed on their territories, consider themselves distinct from other sectors of the societies now prevailing in those territories, or parts of them. They form at present non-dominant sectors of society and are determined to preserve, develop and transmit to future generations their ancestral territories, and their ethnic identity, as the basis of their continued existence as peoples, in accordance with their own cultural patterns, social institutions and legal systems. Source: J. Martinez Cobo (United Nations Special Rapporteur), Study of the Problem of Discrimination against Indigenous Populations, 1987  http://unesdoc.unesco.org/images/0013/001356/135656M.pdf (last accessed: January 15, 2016)

## How are Indigenous land rights connected to cultural preservation?

Indigenous cultures are deeply connected to their ancestral lands, territories, and natural environments. Respecting Indigenous Peoples' rights to their lands and their stewardship of these territories supports self-determination and sustains the relationships between people, culture, and the natural world.

Recognizing Indigenous land rights and title also helps address historical and ongoing harms. An important principle is **Free, Prior and Informed Consent (FPIC)**, which recognizes the right of Indigenous Peoples to participate in decisions affecting their lands, resources, and cultural heritage, including the right to give or withhold consent.

Designers should consider whether their projects, clients, or suppliers may affect Indigenous territories or cultural heritage and seek to respect these rights throughout the design process.

Learn more: [UN Human Rights — Consultation and Free, Prior and Informed Consent (FPIC)](https://www.ohchr.org/en/indigenous-peoples/consultation-and-free-prior-and-informed-consent-fpic).

### What are some examples of a design aimed at preserving or nurturing an aboriginal or indigenous culture?

For example, designing the communication materials for a cultural center; organizing or designing materials for an exhibition aimed at promoting a local tradition, or form of art.
